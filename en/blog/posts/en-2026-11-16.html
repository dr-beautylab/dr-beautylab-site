What is eyebrow semi-permanent makeup?

Eyebrow semi-permanent makeup is a cosmetic procedure that deposits pigment into the upper layer of the skin to create the appearance of fuller or more defined eyebrows. The pigment is placed near the surface of the skin rather than deep into the dermis, which is why the color result changes gradually over time through exfoliation and natural fading. Depending on the style chosen, the result can resemble fine hair strokes, a soft powder-like shading, or a combination of both.

What should I expect during the healing process?

A scab, also called a crust, typically forms over the treated area within 3 to 7 days after the procedure. The scab usually sheds naturally within 7 to 14 days, and color settling and stabilization generally occur over about 3 to 4 weeks. A touch-up is commonly recommended around 4 to 6 weeks after the first session, though the overall color result and how long it lasts can vary significantly depending on individual skin condition and lifestyle.
